# 🧠 Topic: AI-Era Fullstack Interview Landscape

> [!abstract] Executive Summary (ELI5)
> AI writes code now. So interviews are changing — they ask less "write a function" and more "design a system," "evaluate AI output," "architect with AI." This research maps what fullstack developers are being asked in 2026 and how to prepare.

---

## 🎯 The "Why"
- **Problem Solved:** Unclear what to study and build when AI disrupts the traditional interview bar. Used to be: algorithms + coding + basic system design. Now: ???
- **Goal:** Understand the drift — where are interviews going? Where is development going? Then build learning and projects backwards from that.
- **Context:** Transitioning from "pattern-driven projects" to "interview-driven learning."

---

## 🔍 Research Questions

### Primary Questions
1. **What do fullstack interviews look like in 2026?**
   - What are the standard blocks? (System Design, Coding, Behavioral, Architecture?)
   - What is the weight of each block?
2. **How has AI changed the interview bar?**
   - Are coding questions still relevant when AI writes code?
   - What new questions appear? (AI evaluation, prompt engineering, AI architecture?)
   - What skills are now table stakes vs. differentiators?
3. **Where is the industry drifting?**
   - Are companies hiring differently?
   - What do senior engineers need to know that juniors don't when AI is in the loop?
4. **What should I study and build?**
   - Map interview topics → learning priorities → project ideas

### Secondary Questions
- What are the most common fullstack system design questions now?
- How do AI-native architectures (RAG, agents, MCP) appear in interviews?
- What is the "AI engineer" interview track vs. traditional fullstack?
- Are take-home assignments still a thing? What do they look like?
