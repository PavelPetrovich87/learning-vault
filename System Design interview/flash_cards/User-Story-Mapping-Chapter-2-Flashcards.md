# 🎴 User Story Mapping: Plan to Build Less (Chapter 2)

In **Product Development**, what is the critical distinction between **Output** and **Outcome**?
?
- **Output:** The software built and shipped (features, lines of code, release version).
- **Outcome:** The benefit derived by the user or the specific change in behavior that occurs *because* they used the software.

---

What is **Jeff Patton's** "good" definition of a **Minimum Viable Product (MVP)**?
?
The smallest product release that successfully achieves its **desired outcomes** for a specific set of users. It is subjective and depends on who you are trying to thrill.
<!--SR:!2026-05-06,14,230-->

---

According to **Eric Ries**, how should a **Minimum Viable Product (MVP)** be defined in the context of high uncertainty?
?
As the smallest thing you can create or do to **prove or disprove a risky assumption**. It is an experiment meant for learning, not just a smaller version of the final product.
<!--SR:!2026-05-04,6,190-->

In **User Story Mapping**, how does the "Slicing" pattern (using the blue line) assist in release planning?
?
It provides a horizontal visual boundary:
- **Above the line:** "Essential" tasks required for the current target outcome.
- **Below the line:** "Enhancements" or "Later" tasks that can be deferred to maintain the release date.
<!--SR:!2026-05-03,19,250-->

---

Why is the **HiPPO (Highest Paid Person's Opinion)** method considered a prioritization anti-pattern?
?
Because it relies on organizational status rather than **user data**, **risky assumptions**, or **end-to-end journey validation**. It often leads to building "pet features" that don't contribute to a viable outcome.
<!--SR:!2026-05-01,17,250-->

---

In **Story Mapping**, how should "Scope Creep" be re-framed to improve team morale and product quality?
?
As **"Growth of Understanding."** New requirements aren't errors; they are evidence that the team is discovering the "holes" in their initial thinking, which must then be managed via reprioritization and slicing.
<!--SR:!2026-05-22,24,230-->

---

What is the **"Flat Backlog Trap"** when coordinating multiple teams?
?
When teams work from independent, prioritized lists, they often miss **cross-team dependencies**. This results in Team A finishing "high priority" work while Team B's "low priority" dependency (which Team A needs) remains untouched, delaying the entire release.
